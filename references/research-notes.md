# Research basis

Sources checked on 13 October 2026. These notes support the skill; do not append them to an employee's review.

## Performance review guidance

[U.S. Department of Commerce: Writing Up Your Accomplishments](https://www.commerce.gov/hr/employees/performance/write-up)

This HR guide recommends concise accomplishment statements linked to performance expectations. It distinguishes activities from outcomes, asks writers to explain impact, and suggests quality, quantity, timeliness, and cost as measures. It also recommends identifying individual contributions within team work, reporting completed milestones, including customer appreciation, and capturing significant unplanned work. The skill adopts these principles. The guide's local preference for omitting “I” is not universal; this skill defaults to first person unless the employee's form says otherwise.

[Lattice: Tips for Writing a Strong Self-Evaluation](https://lattice.com/articles/tips-for-writing-a-strong-self-evaluation-plus-specific-examples-to-make-yours-shine)

This guide connects evidence to goals, competencies, company values, and business outcomes. The skill uses that distinction to separate results (What) from demonstrated behaviors (How). General self-evaluation advice can also include development areas; this skill omits that material by default to honor the requested positive achievements format.

## ASD-STE100

[Official ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group site](https://www.asd-ste100.org/)

[Official ASD-STE100 Issue 9 text](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)

The official site identifies Issue 9, dated 15 January 2025. STE is a controlled language for technical documentation with writing rules and a controlled dictionary. Word choice involves approved meanings and parts of speech, with provisions for technical terms. Its clarity principles support short, concrete sentences and consistent terminology.

The performance-review workflow is a domain adaptation. It does not contain the controlled dictionary and cannot establish full compliance on style alone. Its 15–20-word target and review-specific output format are editorial choices, not claims about mandatory STE rules. For strict compliance, consult the applicable official standard rather than assuming ordinary plain English is sufficient.
